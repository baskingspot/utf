# Transcoding wave 2: AVX-512 kernels and the gaps against simdutf, 2026-10-31

Wave 2 of `src/convert.zig` and `src/length.zig`: AVX-512 kernels, runtime dispatch, the AVX2 and SSE gaps from [baseline-20261031.md](baseline-20261031.md), and the big-endian test fixes. Measured against simdutf 9.2.1 at each ISA level with `just bench-compare` (harness), and against wave 1 (`7d7cb56`) and simdutf with an interleaved A/B driver.

- Machine: Amp orb, KVM guest, Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake server, family 6 model 106, AVX-512 with VBMI2), 2 cores × 2 threads; every timed process pinned to CPU 3, builds pinned to CPUs 0-1.
- Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast. `native` is `simd.isa == .avx512` with the `v512` kernels, `x86_64_v3` AVX2, `x86_64_v2` SSE, `baseline` generic; none dispatch (`dispatch=false` or a native CPU), so each build runs one ISA's kernels.
- Harness, 3 rounds at the harness's default time per row, the 49 conversion and length operations (listed in [final.sh](convert-20261031/final.sh)): native against simdutf's `icelake`; `cpu=x86_64_v3 dispatch=false kernel=haswell`; `cpu=x86_64_v2 dispatch=false kernel=westmere`. All three runs are the final code on the harness at `0c14f7b`, which calls each function out of line ([final.sh](convert-20261031/final.sh)). Per-corpus tables from [corpus.py](convert-20261031/corpus.py): utf ÷ simdutf, median of the rounds per row, the generated `ascii-N` and `latin1-N` rows as a geomean over N from 16 B to 1 MiB.
- A/B driver: [ab.zig](convert-20261031/ab.zig) links this tree (`new`), wave 1 (`old`), and simdutf into one binary and interleaves arms sample by sample; built by [build.sh](convert-20261031/build.sh), run by [abfinal.sh](convert-20261031/abfinal.sh) at 16, 64, 256, 4096, and 65536 bytes of UTF-8 per corpus (Latin-Lipsum as ascii, French Wikipedia as latin, and the harness's Russian, Chinese, Emoji, Korean, and Hindi lipsum texts and its `latin1-65536` text as latin1t), 6 samples. Raw output (gzipped) and benchstat (`-col /arm`) in [ab/](convert-20261031/ab/); ratios from [abratio.py](convert-20261031/abratio.py) and [abrows.py](convert-20261031/abrows.py).
- Raw harness output in [native/](convert-20261031/native/), [avx2/](convert-20261031/avx2/), [sse/](convert-20261031/sse/): `summary.txt`, `benchstat.txt`, `raw-utf.txt`, `raw-simdutf.txt`, `rss.txt`, `config.txt`.

## Takeaways

- **Against simdutf, harness**: geomean over 701 rows 1.14 of `icelake` (wave 1: 0.85 over the same operations, on the baseline's harness), 1.33 of `haswell` (1.09), 1.27 of `westmere`. By input: the named corpora (whole files, 5-450 KiB) 1.25 of `icelake` (lowest row 0.88), 1.37 of `haswell` (0.74), 1.30 of `westmere` (0.80); the generated rows at 16, 64, 256 B, 4 KiB, 64 KiB, and 1 MiB 1.04, 0.98, 1.00, 1.30, 1.07, 1.04 of `icelake`, 1.21, 1.42, 1.50, 1.48, 1.21, 1.06 of `haswell`, and 1.15, 1.31, 1.39, 1.33, 1.26, 1.06 of `westmere`. The checked forms' 16-64 B rows still carry about 4 ns of the harness's own (below).
- **Against simdutf, A/B driver**, geomean over every op, corpus, and form: native 1.25 of `icelake` at 4 KiB and 1.24 at 64 KiB; AVX2 1.37 and 1.32 of `haswell`; SSE 1.29 and 1.26 of `westmere`. Below 4 KiB the driver favours us (2.0-2.5× at 16-64 B): its arms can inline our function, while simdutf's calls go through its dispatch pointer.
- **Against wave 1**, A/B driver, from 4 KiB: native 1.77 (4 KiB) and 1.54 (64 KiB). UTF-8 to UTF-16 1.8-3.6× on non-Latin text (Cyrillic 2.9, emoji 3.4-3.6, CJK 1.8-1.9), UTF-16 to UTF-8 1.5-1.7× on CJK, Korean, and Hindi, UTF-32 to UTF-8 1.4-1.5×, UTF-8 to Latin-1 on Latin-1 text 8-9×, Latin-1 to UTF-8 3.5×, UTF-16 to UTF-32 on emoji 6-7×, the checked byte swap on emoji 7-10×. AVX2 1.30 and 1.27: UTF-16 to UTF-8 on CJK 1.9-2.0×, Korean and Hindi 1.5×; UTF-8 to UTF-16 1.4-2.1×; UTF-16 to UTF-32 on emoji 3.5×. SSE 1.21 and 1.22. Generic (`baseline`) 1.06 and 1.00 of wave 1, 2.1× the scalar code overall.
- **Short inputs.** Called out of line with the result read field by field ([short-ab.zig](convert-20261031/short-ab.zig), valid forms, ns, new against wave 1): AVX2 is within ±15% of wave 1 from 8 B to 256 B and faster at 1000 B. Native is faster at every length that is not a multiple of 16 bytes (`validUtf8ToUtf16le` at 24 B 4.8 against 9.3, non-ASCII `validLatin1ToUtf8` at 64 B 7.0 against 12.5) and slower on exact multiples of 16 ASCII bytes (`validLatin1ToUtf8` at 16 B 7.6 against 3.2, at 64 B 5.0 against 3.2), where wave 1's 16-byte path left no tail and the AVX-512 kernels load their 64-byte constants first. Sending inputs under 64 bytes to the 16-byte paths was slower on every other length (24 B Latin-1 to UTF-8 9.2 against 3.7 ns) and was dropped.
- **A stall in every checked conversion.** `tail`, which joins the SIMD part's progress to the scalar tail's `Result`, chose between two built results; LLVM wrote both fields of the chosen one to the stack with an 8-byte and a 1-byte store and copied the pair out with one 16-byte load, which cannot forward from them: about 6 ns a call, on every ISA and in wave 1 too (the baseline's "16 bytes 0.44-0.50 of `haswell`" rows). Building the one result from scalars removes it. Out of line, the result read field by field ([tail-ab.zig](convert-20261031/tail-ab.zig), [tail-ab.txt](convert-20261031/tail-ab.txt), ns, after against before): `latin1ToUtf16le` at 16 B 4.4 against 10.1 native and 3.5 against 9.5 AVX2; `utf8ToUtf16le` 5.3 against 10.7 and 5.2 against 10.2; every checked conversion 1.2-2.8× at 16-64 B and 1.1-1.9× at 256 B, level from 4 KiB; the valid forms, which do not use `tail`, unchanged. The A/B driver did not show it, since its arms inline our functions and read the fields directly; the harness's own whole-result read had the same stall and was fixed in `0c14f7b`. The harness has one more: its `written()` turns a checked `Result` into its `Out` union by choosing one of two variants, the same pattern. Modelled in [harness-written-ab.zig](convert-20261031/harness-written-ab.zig) ([output](convert-20261031/harness-written-ab.txt)): `latin1ToUtf16le` on 16 bytes, called out of line, 9.0 ns through a chosen variant and 4.9 ns with one variant built from scalars (valid form 3.3 ns). So the checked forms' 16-64 B rows in the tables below, about 0.5-0.6 of simdutf, understate them by about 4 ns a call.
- **Memory.** No allocation. Peak RSS (`rss.txt` at each level): the same as wave 1's to 0.1 MiB, 0.1-0.5 MiB over Zig std's where std has the operation (std itself moved 0.3 MiB between runs), and 3.3-3.8 MiB under simdutf's.
- **Correctness.** `just oracle 200000` 0 mismatches under `icelake` and `fallback`. Tests at every `-Dcpu` level, in Debug, and on aarch64 and aarch64_be under qemu; each differential test runs at every dispatch level the CPU has.

## What moved the numbers

Each kept change was measured with an interleaved A/B; the dropped ones are listed too.

- AVX-512 kernels, following Clausecker and Lemire, "Transcoding Unicode Characters with AVX-512 Instructions" (2023), and Lemire and Muła (2022). UTF-8 to UTF-16 and UTF-32 decode 64 bytes per step and compress the lanes at character ends (`vpcompressb`/`w`/`d`); UTF-16 and UTF-32 to UTF-8 build each code point's bytes with `vpmultishiftqb` and `vpermb` and compress them.
- UTF-8 to Latin-1 decodes at every continuation byte from a load one byte back, so steps are a fixed 64 bytes with no loop-carried shift, and backs up a byte when a block ends in a lead byte: Latin-1 text 8-9× wave 1 at 4-64 KiB.
- Latin-1 to UTF-8 makes each byte's lead and continuation byte in byte lanes and interleaves them with `vpermt2b`, then compresses; no per-half ASCII branch.
- The checked byte swap checks that a unit is a high surrogate exactly when the next is a low one, a 64-byte block at a time, then takes the rest as the block that ends with the input, then 8 units, then 4: emoji from 0.22-0.24 of `icelake` (baseline) to 1.42-1.77; inputs under 33 units no longer go to the scalar code (checked, CJK at 64 B: 24.9 ns before the short passes, 7.2 after, wave 1 7.6).
- Masked loads and stores for tails only when the 64 bytes stay in one page; across a page, a stack buffer in an out-of-line cold function. Inline, its 64-byte-aligned buffer gave every caller a realigned stack frame and five saved registers: Latin-1 to UTF-16 and valid UTF-32 to Latin-1 1.2-1.5× faster at 5-31 units ([align-ab.zig](convert-20261031/align-ab.zig) with `N` set).
- The Latin-1 widening and narrowing loops and the byte swap start long inputs (from 16 blocks) with one whole block, then step to the output's 64-byte boundary. Over 16 combinations of input and output offset ([align-ab.zig](convert-20261031/align-ab.zig)): Latin-1 to UTF-16 at 64 Ki units 4905 to 3053 ns (1.61×), at 4 Ki 1.69×; Latin-1 to UTF-32 1.43× and 1.79×; valid UTF-32 to Latin-1 1.13× at 64 Ki; valid byte swap 1.22× at 4 Ki. Aligning the input instead of the output made valid UTF-32 to Latin-1 0.78× in one placement and was dropped.
- AVX2 and SSE: UTF-16 and UTF-32 to UTF-8 encode BMP text in 16-bit lanes, unpacking to bytes with `punpck` and indexing the pack table with `pext` where BMI2 is there; UTF-16 to UTF-32 has a 32-unit BMP sprint.
- Lengths: on AVX-512 a block's count is a mask popcount (one port-5 operation per 64 bytes, where widening the mask to add it takes two); loads are aligned from the second block. On AVX2 and SSE the aligned head pays from 16 vectors on, not 2: at 256 B the head's extra reduction cost 20-35% ([length-ab/](convert-20261031/length-ab/), new against the 2-vector threshold: 1.26× AVX2 and 1.31× SSE at 256 B, 1.00 from 4 KiB). Lengths in the harness, by corpus: `countUtf8` 1.44-1.60 of `icelake`, `countUtf16le/be` 1.09-1.73, `utf8FromUtf32` 1.22-1.38, `utf8FromUtf16le/be` 0.94-1.35; against `westmere` the counts are 1.16-2.64 and `utf8FromUtf16le/be` and `utf8FromUtf32` 0.83-0.96.
- Dropped: steps of 256 or 1024 units on the generic path (neutral to −15%); accumulating counts in vectors on AVX-512 (slower than popcounts); a 4-block ASCII check in UTF-8 to Latin-1 (slower than one per block on French).
- Generic path: its checked forms are within ±10% of an out-of-line call to the scalar code on text with no ASCII; wave 1's "up to 25% under scalar" compared with an inlined scalar arm.
