# v0.2.2 under Zig 0.17: the zig-0.17 fixes main never took, 2026-11-05

Ghostty measured `utf` v0.2.2 (`026ba6b`) on an Apple M4 Pro 2.0× slower than simdutf on emoji and 1.5× on mixed UTF-8, where the `zig-0.17` branch (`4270ca9`) had been faster than simdutf. The cause: three commits from `zig-0.17` that work around Zig 0.17 codegen never reached `main`. When `main` took Zig 0.17 support (`9b4dcff`, `c47e707`), it ported the Apple-core and base64 commits of `zig-0.17` (`5266773`, `39e794f`, and `0331490` match `efe6c38`, `13a39c2`, and `395d38f` patch for patch), but not these three:

- `8bc7ac2` → `497ac96`: `utf8DecodeBlock`'s `tops = @bitCast(@shuffle(...))` into `[64]u16`, which Zig 0.17 lowers as a loop that shifts an `i1024` once per lane ([bitcast-array](bitcast-array-20261101.md)).
- `eb10f9c` → `3e2c8bd`: ten array `@bitCast`s in base64.zig, through `simd.split` and `simd.join` ([bitcast-sweep](bitcast-sweep-20261101.md)).
- `7d9f4b5` → `d891cf7`: NEON `storeWide`, `storeWideUnits`, and `simd.lookup16`, whose array casts became a lane move per byte ([neon-apple](neon-apple-20261101.md), step 1).

They apply to `main` unchanged; this branch is the three, with the same content and messages.

## Apple Silicon

- Machine: Amp Mac satellite, Apple M4 Pro (virtual, `VirtualMac2,1`, 6 vCPUs), macOS 27.2. Zig 0.17.0 from Homebrew, ReleaseFast, `aarch64-macos` with the native CPU. simdutf: Ghostty's vendored copy at `e95bed633` (`pkg/simdutf/vendor/`), compiled in the same build.
- Harness: [neon-apple-20261101/harness/](neon-apple-20261101/harness/), unchanged: Ghostty's `DecodeUTF8` and base64 decode as shims, one process with simdutf and three `utf` arms, each row on every arm back to back in a rotating order, round 0 sizing the loops, 20 ms samples; `ratio.py` gives the median over rounds of each round's simdutf ÷ arm (above 1: the arm is faster), with the range. The `cpu: Apple M4 Max` header line is hard-coded in `main.cpp`. Only `build.zig.zon`'s paths changed: `base` is `4270ca9`, `new` this branch, `alt` v0.2.2.
- Correctness: `ab test`, 300 000 random inputs per operation through `new` against simdutf, 0 mismatches. The package's tests on the Mac in Debug and ReleaseFast, and ReleaseFast with `-Dcpu=generic` (the NEON paths other cores take): all pass.

The UTF-16 rows, against `4270ca9` (the vendored simdutf has no UTF-16): this branch 0.99–1.01, v0.2.2 0.47–0.81 (`latin-4096` 0.48, `emoji-4095` 0.62, `cjk-4096` 0.80). Rows of 1–16 bytes: one cost, which `d891cf7` brings back as it did on `zig-0.17` ([neon-apple](neon-apple-20261101.md), step 1, 0.7–1 ns): `ascii-1` is 1.38 of simdutf on this branch and 1.67 on v0.2.2, about 0.7 ns a call; `ascii-8` 1.48 and 1.51; the other short rows overlap. This branch matches `4270ca9` on every row (medians of the paired ratios 0.96–1.05), and v0.2.2 is 7.3× slower on emoji (Ghostty's end-to-end 2.0× includes the terminal's own work), 2× slower on two-byte, box, and Korean text, and 1.2–1.5× on CJK and Japanese.

Which commit fixes which ([ratio3-v022.txt](zig017-port-20261105/apple/ratio3-v022.txt), 8 rounds, arms against v0.2.2): with only `497ac96`, emoji is 7.5–7.8× v0.2.2's speed and every other row within 4% of it; adding `3e2c8bd` changes nothing on UTF-8 to UTF-32; `d891cf7` brings the rest. In the harness's assembly, v0.2.2 has 438 moves from a general register into a vector lane where `4270ca9` and this branch have 52, and 46 `extr` where they have none: the per-lane stores of `storeWide`'s `[16]u32` and the `i1024` shifts.

## x86-64

- **v0.2.2 under Zig 0.17 had the emoji regression on x86 too**, 10–11× on the SSE, AVX2, and AVX-512 kernels without VBMI. CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI (Ice Lake, Zen 4) take another decoder and were never affected, which is why Ghostty's Zen 4 numbers were fine. Base64 with whitespace under Zig 0.17: `Base64DecodeMime` at 64 and 256 characters 18–39% faster at `x86_64_v3` and `x86_64_v4`, 6.5% at 64 characters at `x86_64_v2`; clean base64 unchanged.
- **Zig 0.16: unchanged**, apart from `x86_64_v4` emoji 17% faster. No row slower beyond its noise except `Base64DecodeMime/random-64` at `x86_64_v3`, +2.7% (p=0.043, ±18–24% intervals), and at 16 characters under Zig 0.17, +3.2% (p=0.007); both under a nanosecond.
- Memory: unchanged; no allocation.

## Regression check

Not added. What failed was the port, not a test: every test passes on v0.2.2, and the outputs are right, only slow. A check would have to be a timing (a floor against simdutf on a CI runner, which the GitHub runners' noise makes loose but a 7–11× drop would still trip) or a count of lane moves in the emitted assembly (exact, but tied to one Zig and LLVM version). Either is the owner's call.
