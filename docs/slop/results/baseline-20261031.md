# Baseline for wave 2: every operation against simdutf, 2026-10-31

The first run of the wave-2 harness: all 66 operations in `bench/ops.tsv` (validation of ASCII, UTF-8, UTF-16LE/BE, and UTF-32 with and without errors; every conversion among UTF-8, UTF-16LE/BE, UTF-32, and Latin-1, checked and valid; the length functions and code-point counts; base64 with both alphabets, strict and stop-before-partial decoding, and 76-column MIME lines), 877 rows each for simdutf and this package, after wave 1 and runtime dispatch. It is the gap list for the wave-2 performance pass.

- Machine: Amp orb, KVM guest, Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake server, family 6 model 106, AVX-512 with VBMI2), 4 vCPUs; every arm pinned to CPU 3.
- simdutf 9.2.1 (nixpkgs); Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast; Rust 1.99.0 with `simdutf8` 0.1.5, `encoding_rs` 0.8.44, `base64-simd` 0.8.0, `-C target-cpu=native`; Go 1.26.10.
- Runs, 6 rounds each at `BENCHTIME_MS=100`, about 36 minutes apiece:
  - **native**: `just bench-compare`, the package built for the host CPU (`simd.isa == .avx512`) against simdutf's runtime pick, `icelake`.
  - **AVX2**: `just cpu=x86_64_v3 dispatch=false kernel=haswell bench-compare`, an x86-64-v3 build (`simd.isa == .avx2`) against simdutf's `haswell` kernel, like for like.
- Both runs timed the package at `7d7cb56`, before `0511c0e` merged runtime dispatch; the AVX2 run's `config.txt` predates the `dispatch` variable. Between the two commits only `validate.utf8` and `validate.utf8WithErrors` changed, and only for builds that dispatch: native and `dispatch=false` builds call the kernel directly. An interleaved A/B of the native `bench-utf` built from each (3 rounds of both validation operations, 30 rows) gave geomean 19.90 against 19.90 GB/s, no row significant, so both runs stand for `0511c0e`. On `0511c0e` an `x86_64_v3` build dispatches to the AVX-512 kernels unless `dispatch=false`.
- Raw output in [baseline-20261031/](baseline-20261031/): `native/` and `avx2/`, each with `raw-<arm>.txt`, `benchstat.txt` (every row, simdutf as base), `summary.txt` (`just bench-summary`), and `config.txt`. `native/rss.txt` was taken after the timing runs, once the Zig arms allocated each input's output buffer per row as the other arms do; before, they held every buffer at once, which overstated their RSS on the large-output operations (latin1-to-utf32 12.3 MiB, now 7.4).
- The harness then inlined the Zig and Rust arms' calls into the timing loop, which overstated `utf`'s and Rust's rows from 16 to 256 bytes, UTF-8 validation of 16 bytes by 2×; see [harness-fix-20261031.md](harness-fix-20261031.md). Rows from 4 KiB moved at most 5%.
- Equivalence: in both runs `utf` 877, `std` 156, `rust` 248, and `go` 141 rows match simdutf's checksums; std skips 52 operations, Rust 44, Go 53, for want of a library function.

## Takeaways

Ratios are this package's throughput over simdutf's, geomean over an operation's inputs; above 1 is faster. Overall: 0.81 against `icelake`, 1.15 against `haswell`. So the AVX2 kernels are level or ahead of simdutf's on most operations, and nearly every gap against `icelake` is AVX-512 kernels this package does not have yet.

- **validate.zig.** UTF-8 leads both (1.13 and 1.20 native, 1.48 and 1.36 AVX2); its slowest row is 1 MiB of ASCII at 0.70 of `icelake` (45.5 against 65.1 GB/s). ASCII and UTF-16 validation lead `haswell` (1.48–2.31) and UTF-32 trails it (0.87, Korean 0.64; the error form leads at 1.49); against `icelake` all three trail at 0.47–0.83: on long input `icelake` validates UTF-16 at 135–152 GB/s to ours 45–53, UTF-32 at 98 to 46, and ASCII on the 65 KB `latin` text at 147 to 52. The native ASCII validator (36.98 GB/s geomean) is slower than our own AVX2 build (45.35); native UTF-32 is faster (49.6 against 29.6). The `-err` forms on 16 bytes are the short-input gap: UTF-16BE 0.28, UTF-32 0.31 of `icelake` (1.25 against 4.51 GB/s, 3.33 against 10.87), 0.63 of `haswell`.
- **convert.zig.** Against `haswell` most conversions lead or are level; the gaps are UTF-16 to UTF-8 on CJK and Hindi (0.50–0.52, 2.5 against 5.0 GB/s), UTF-32 to Latin-1 (0.45–0.52 from 4 KiB, 31–40 against 69–79 GB/s), the checked UTF-16 byte swap on emoji (0.43–0.47), and 16-byte Latin-1 and UTF-16-to-Latin-1 rows (0.44–0.50). Against `icelake`: UTF-8 to Latin-1 on Latin-1 text is the largest gap in the table, 0.11–0.19 (1.7–2.2 against 10–20 GB/s; 1.36–1.49 of `haswell`, so `icelake` has an AVX-512 kernel for it), then Latin-1 to UTF-8 on the same text at 0.22–0.30 (2.7–3.9 against 12.4–16.9), UTF-16 to UTF-32 and the checked byte swap on emoji (0.22–0.28), and UTF-8 to UTF-16 on Arabic and Hebrew (0.36). UTF-32 to UTF-8 leads `icelake` (1.20; emoji 2.3–2.8×) and is level with `haswell` (1.01, Chinese 0.64). The checked forms from Latin-1 to UTF-16 and UTF-32 cost about 20% over the valid forms (`latin1-to-utf16le` 0.77 against `valid-` 0.97 of `icelake`), where simdutf has one function for both.
- **length.zig.** 0.81–1.28 of `icelake` and 0.78–2.01 of `haswell`. The code-point counts and `utf16FromUtf32` lead (`countUtf16le` 95 against 40–46 GB/s on long text); `utf8FromLatin1` trails both (0.81, 0.78; 56 against 118 GB/s at 64 KiB native), and `utf8FromUtf32` and `utf8FromUtf16le/be` sit at 0.86–0.92 of `icelake` and 0.90–1.01 of `haswell`.
- **base64.zig.** Against `haswell`: encode 1.11–1.15, decode 0.97–1.04, MIME 0.77. Against `icelake`: encode 0.74–0.78, decode 0.66–0.71 (slowest 4 KiB, 0.40–0.41), and MIME 0.50: simdutf decodes 76-column lines at 14.6–15.0 GB/s from 4 KiB to our 5.7–5.9, inside the 10.3–14.2 GB/s the base64 orb measured. The URL alphabet, strict, and stop-before-partial modes cost nothing over the default in either library.
- **Short inputs** (16 bytes, AVX2 run): geomean 1.16 of `haswell` over 83 rows; the 21 rows under 0.75 are Latin-1 to and from UTF-16 and UTF-32 (0.44–0.50), `utf16FromUtf32` (0.57), the counts and `-err` validators on UTF-16 and UTF-32 (0.63–0.69).
- **Other arms.** Rust's `simdutf8` is level with us on UTF-8 validation (1.00, 0.96) and its `is_ascii` near (0.97, 0.78 for the error form); every other operation leads Rust, Go, and Zig's std, by 1.15× (Rust base64 decode) to 233× (std's `calcUtf16LeLen`).
- **Memory.** No allocation in any operation; peak RSS is simdutf's minus 3.2–3.7 MiB on every operation, and Zig std's plus 0.1–0.3 MiB where std has the operation (the larger binary). Rust is 1.0–5.9 MiB over ours, Go 1.5–8.8.
