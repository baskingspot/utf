# Short calls, wave 5, 2026-11-01

Wave 5's first job: what a 16–256-byte call costs beyond its kernel, on `39088d1` (wave 4 merged). Three changes, `src/convert.zig` and `src/base64.zig` only; no `@setRuntimeSafety(false)` (plan decision 5).

- Machine: Amp orb, KVM guest, Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake server, AVX-512 with VBMI2), 2 cores × 2 threads; every timed process pinned to CPU 3, builds to CPUs 0–1, nothing built while anything was timed.
- Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast, simdutf 9.2.1. `native` is `-Dcpu=native` (the AVX-512 kernels inlined into their callers); `dispatched` is `-Dcpu=baseline`, whose AVX-512 tier object is called through a dispatch slot. Neither build can run the AVX2 or SSE tier objects on this host: the dispatcher picks AVX-512, and nothing overrides it.
- Harness A/Bs: [ab.sh](short-20261101/ab.sh), the harness's method with any arms: every arm a `bench-utf serve` (or `bench-simdutf serve`, forced to `icelake`) on CPU 3, each row timed on every arm back to back in an order that rotates by row and round, fresh processes each round, round 0 sizing the loops and discarded, then `bench/summary.awk` (median per-round ratio, 95% interval, `~` where it holds 1). Arms are `bench-utf` built by [hb.sh](short-20261101/hb.sh) from `39088d1` (`old`), `66fa49c` (`w66`, before wave 4's calls work), each step of this branch, and the final tree (`final`). [sizes.py](short-20261101/sizes.py) gives an arm's geomean by input size. Ratios are the first arm ÷ the other; above 1 the first is faster. 7 rounds for the conversion runs (234 rows: every conversion at 16, 64, 256, and 4096 code units of ASCII and Latin-1 text, and every Latin-1 to UTF-8 row), 9 for the base64 and Latin-1 runs.
- Per-call loop: [calls/](short-20261101/calls/) (from [calls-20261031.md](calls-20261031.md)), each public function called out of line 1 million times per loop on ASCII letters or, with `LATIN=1`, bytes 0xA0–0xFF, TSC ticks per call (2.6 a nanosecond), the minimum over 15 loops and 3 rotating runs ([callsab.sh](short-20261101/callsab.sh)); base64 inputs are padded, as the harness's are, at 24, 88, and 344 characters. `calls.cpp` gained a `latin1ToUtf8` case for simdutf. The Zig loop costs 3.9–4.7 ticks a call and simdutf's, through the PLT, 7.0.
- Code: [prologue.py](short-20261101/prologue.py) lists each tier export's pushes before its first branch; [fndiff.py](short-20261101/fndiff.py) says whether a function's instructions are the same in two binaries once addresses are normalized; [trace.py](short-20261101/trace.py) single-steps one call under gdb and prints every instruction it runs.

## Takeaways

- **The saved registers were real but small.** On `39088d1`, 18 AVX-512 conversion exports saved 3–6 callee-saved registers before their length test (16 saved six); now none saves more than one before it. Dispatched, 16-unit conversions are 1.05× `old` (geomean of 56 rows; UTF-32 to UTF-16 and UTF-8 1.23–1.25×, UTF-8 to anything 1.08–1.16×, Latin-1 to UTF-8 1.13–1.17×), 64-unit 1.005×, 256-unit 0.987×, 4096-unit 1.004×. Native builds keep the kernels whole (split, 64–256-byte rows lost 9–12%): 1.02× at 16 units, 0.96–0.99× above, inside the layout noise of rows whose code did not change.
- **`base64.decode` with AVX-512 takes inputs under 64 characters with one masked load**, where `loadTail` built the block from integer loads, and its failures out of line: 16 bytes (24 characters) 1.20× `old` native and dispatched, 64–256 bytes 1.00–1.05×. Per call, dispatched: 35.3 to 27.0 ticks at 24 characters. The native regression since `66fa49c` (0.96 at 16–256 bytes) is gone: 1.20, 1.00, 0.99 of `66fa49c`. Its long path stays inline and keeps its six saved registers: out of line, as `validate.utf8`'s is, 88 and 344 characters cost 7–12 ticks more a call (harness 0.83 and 0.90 of `old` at 64 and 256 bytes), more than the registers saved at 24.
- **AVX-512 Latin-1 to UTF-8 at 256 bytes: 1.08–1.28×** (harness, against the split alone; per call 1.07–1.22× at 128–1024 bytes), from inlining its block loop into the long path (shared by the checked and valid forms, LLVM had kept it out of line: two prologues and a result through memory) and from testing each block once. The dozen constant loads before the loop were not the cost: moving the tail out of the function took them to seven and changed nothing at 256 bytes (50.9 to 50.5 ticks). Per call, 256 bytes of Latin-1 now take 44.5 ticks dispatched against simdutf's 45.9.
- **The harness's Zig arm costs more per call than its C++ arm.** The same dispatched `latin1ToUtf8` takes 9.0 ns a call in the harness at 16 bytes and 25.4 ns at 256 bytes of Latin-1, and 5.8 and 17.1 ns in the per-call loop; simdutf's arm takes 8.9 and 17.6 ns in the harness and 9.0 and 17.6 ns in its per-call loop. testingx's `b.loop()` is a call per iteration that saves seven registers and returns its error union through memory; adding a call like it to the per-call loop costs 2.5 ticks at 16 bytes and 7 at 256, part of the gap. Measured on this function only, but the loop is every row's: a 16–256-byte row under simdutf by a few nanoseconds a call is not evidence about the kernel until the loop is fixed ([The harness loop](#the-harness-loop)).
- **Identical code moves up to 23%.** `validUtf32ToLatin1`'s export is byte for byte the same in `old` and `final` (`fndiff.py`) and read 0.87–0.89 of `old` at 16–256 values dispatched; `validUtf8ToLatin1`'s long path differs from `old`'s export only in register names and read 0.77 at 4 KiB of Latin-1 in the first split's run. Zig cannot align functions or loops, so a row under about 15% from one build to another is not attributed to a change.
- **Memory**: nothing allocates; the tier objects grew by the out-of-line long paths (the Peak RSS in [baseline-wave5-20261101.md](baseline-wave5-20261101.md) is within 0.1 MiB of the final baseline's).

## 1. Long paths out of line in the tier objects (convert)

LLVM saves a function's callee-saved registers on entry, before any length test, when any path in it needs them, so a 16-byte call into an AVX-512 conversion saved and restored up to six registers for a loop it never ran. Wave 4 fixed it for `validate.utf8` ([calls-20261031.md](calls-20261031.md)): where `dispatch.exported` (a tier object, whose kernels are called out of line anyway), the long path is a `noinline` function and its `Result` comes back through `dispatch.pack`/`unpack` in registers (returned whole it goes through memory, and the caller's load of `err` stalls, about 7 ns). `longPath(apart, f, ct, src, dst)` in `convert.zig` is that recipe for any kernel: it calls `f` inline unless `apart and dispatch.exported`.

The short path runs first, in each kernel's `Impl`, and the long path (`*Long`: the AVX-512 loop and the scalar tail) goes out of line:

- UTF-8 to UTF-16 and UTF-32: up to 64 ASCII bytes (moved out of `utf8ToUnitsAvx512`, where it ran after the prologue).
- UTF-8 to Latin-1: up to 64 bytes in one masked step (`utf8ToLatin1Step`).
- UTF-16 to UTF-8: up to 32 ASCII units (moved out of `utf16ToUtf8Avx512`).
- Checked UTF-32 to UTF-8 and UTF-16: up to 16 values in one masked step (`utf32Invalid`, `utf32ToUtf16Step`, shared with the loops).
- Checked UTF-32 to Latin-1: up to 64 values in steps of 16, the last masked. A masked step for up to 16 alone, as the others have, made 64 values pay the call: 0.87 of `old` dispatched at 64 values; with the steps, 1.12–1.15× that version and 0.97–0.99 of `old`.
- Latin-1 to UTF-8: up to 64 bytes in whole-register stores where `dst` has room for them (`latin1ToUtf8Whole`, no mask or page test); the masked short path stays in the long path for short outputs.

Left whole: the kernels that saved 0–2 registers (UTF-16 to UTF-32 and to Latin-1, the byte swap, Latin-1 to UTF-16 and UTF-32, the valid UTF-32 forms). Split, their 64–256-byte rows lost about 3 ticks for the call; and adding an inline short path to a kernel that saved none made LLVM save six on entry. Native builds keep every kernel whole: there the kernel is inlined into its caller, and split, the first run (`n1`) lost 9–12% at 64–256 bytes.

## 2. `base64.decode`

`decodeWith` trims, then takes a block or more inline (`decodeBlocks`, unchanged) and anything shorter through `decodeShort`: on AVX-512 one `simd.loadShort`, where `loadTail` built a block shorter than 64 bytes from integer loads, then the classify and `finish` it had. A short input that fails (bad characters, a partial chunk, too little room) goes to `decodeShortRest`, `noinline` in every build.

The first version also put the long path out of line in the tier objects, as `validate.utf8`'s is, which took the export from six saved registers to two. It cost more than it saved. Per call, dispatched (`calls/`, ticks):

| characters | `old` | long path out of line | same, trim passed | `final` |
| ---------: | ----: | --------------------: | ----------------: | ------: |
|         24 |  35.3 |                  26.0 |              25.0 |    27.0 |
|         88 |  39.3 |                  48.8 |              43.8 |    36.7 |
|        344 |  48.1 |                  58.9 |              52.0 |    47.0 |

In the harness the out-of-line version read 1.26× the split (whose decode is `old`'s) at 16 bytes and 0.83 and 0.90 at 64 and 256 (`b64-d`). Single-stepped (`trace.py`), one call runs 160 instructions in `old`, 111 out of line, and 120 in `final` at 24 characters, and 187, 240, and 181 at 88: out of line, the trim runs twice, a second prologue saves six registers, and the trim is stored for `decodeStaged`. The 7–12 ticks are more than 53 simple instructions; that was not taken further.

**The native regression since `66fa49c`** (wave 4: 0.92–0.94 at 16–256 bytes; here `old` ÷ `66fa49c` 0.958 at each of 16, 64, and 256, `b64-n`): `base64.decode`'s native code differs between the two (1148 against 1245 instructions after normalizing addresses, with different register allocation) after `1e2d290`'s early return for an empty last block and `345f64c`'s `finish` and `packAll` over any number of vectors, which are no-ops for AVX-512 at the source level. It is codegen, not layout, but was not narrowed to one instruction. The rewrite of the short path took it away: `final` is 1.20, 1.00, and 0.99 of `66fa49c`.

The AVX2 and SSE decode exports still save six registers, for `loadPartial`'s integer loads and the long path; they cannot be timed dispatched on this host.

## 3. AVX-512 Latin-1 to UTF-8

At 256 bytes of Latin-1 the native row was 17.6 ns against simdutf's 14.6 in wave 4, and the suspects were the constants loaded before the loop and the first block tested three times (twice in the ASCII loops, again by the main loop). Three changes, measured in turn per call:

- **The ASCII loops run only after an ASCII first block** (`e4912c7`'s first version): one 64-byte test where Latin-1 text tested its first block in both loops.
- **One test per block.** The main loop enters the encoding loop with the block it loaded and tested; before, the encoding loop loaded and tested it again.
- **`latin1ToUtf8Avx512` inline.** Shared by the checked and valid forms, LLVM kept it out of line, so the long path saved its registers, called it, and it saved six more and returned `{p, o}` through memory.

Tried and dropped on the way: whole blocks only in the loop, with the rest after it, cut the constants loaded before the loop from eleven to seven and changed nothing at 256 bytes of Latin-1 (50.9 to 50.5 ticks), while the long path, now holding the tail, saved six registers instead of three (ASCII at 128–256 bytes 2.5–3.3 ticks slower). The constants are loads off the critical path. Masked stores for the last blocks of an output sized to fit were not tried in the harness, whose outputs have room (2n + 64 bytes).

## The harness loop

The per-call loop and the harness disagree about our short calls and agree about simdutf's (ns a call; harness from the median GB/s of `r3-d`'s paired rounds, dispatched `final` and `icelake`; Latin-1 to UTF-8 unless named):

| row                                    | utf, harness | utf, per-call loop | simdutf, harness | simdutf, per-call loop |
| -------------------------------------- | -----------: | -----------------: | ---------------: | ---------------------: |
| 16 bytes of Latin-1                    |          9.0 |                5.8 |              8.9 |                    9.0 |
| 256 bytes of Latin-1                   |         25.4 |               17.1 |             17.6 |                   17.6 |
| Latin-1 to UTF-16LE, 16 bytes of ASCII |          8.1 |                5.1 |              5.2 |                    5.4 |

`Latin1ToUtf16le/ascii-16` reads 0.64 of `icelake` in the harness, and per call the two take 13.3 and 14.2 ticks: the same 3 ns. The Latin-1 to UTF-8 inputs differ (the harness's Latin-1 text has 2–11 bytes above 0x7F in each 64-byte block, the loop's every byte), which cannot account for 3 ns at 16 bytes, and simdutf's figures agree across the same difference. The harness times each row as `while (try b.loop()) { ...; @call(.never_inline, f, .{ in, out }) }`: testingx's `B.loop` is not inlined, saves seven registers (frame pointer included) on every iteration, increments its counter in memory, and returns its `!bool` through a stack slot; `f` is the arm's wrapper, which calls the public function, which calls through the dispatch slot. A copy of the per-call loop with a `noinline` function like `B.loop`'s fast path called each iteration ([calls/](short-20261101/calls/) is the plain loop; the variant was scratch) took 16 bytes from 13.3 to 15.8 ticks and 256 bytes of Latin-1 from 44.1–44.9 to 51.3–52.0: part of the gap, not all of it. The loop-cost check in [baseline-final-20261031.md](baseline-final-20261031.md) timed a callee that returns at once (1.25 ns for Zig, 1.41 for C++), which does not show it. Until the harness's loop is fixed, a 16–256-byte row under simdutf by less than a few nanoseconds a call is not evidence about the kernel.

## AVX2 and SSE

Not changed. Their conversion exports save 3–6 registers before the first branch too, but that branch is the loop's own length test: they have no short path before the loop, and an input shorter than a vector runs the scalar tail, which uses the registers itself. Splitting would move the pushes, not remove them, and neither tier object can be timed dispatched here.
