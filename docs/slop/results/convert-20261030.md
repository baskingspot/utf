# Transcoding: UTF-8, UTF-16, UTF-32, Latin-1, 2026-10-30

The wave-1 transcoders in `src/convert.zig` and `src/length.zig`, with the scalar reference in `src/scalar_convert.zig`, against simdutf, Zig's std, Rust, and Go (`just bench-compare`), and against simdutf and std on every operation the harness lacks, with the driver in [convert-20261030/driver.zig](convert-20261030/driver.zig). Every number is from one machine; compare within a table only. Speeds on this orb moved by up to 2× between runs and by 10–20% between samples within one run.

- Machine: Amp orb, KVM guest, Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake server, family 6 model 106, AVX-512 with VBMI2), 4 vCPUs.
- Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast. `native` selects `simd.isa == .avx512`, `x86_64_v3` AVX2, `x86_64_v2` SSE, `baseline` generic. Transcoding has no AVX-512 kernels yet: on `avx512` it runs the AVX2 code, with `lookup16` widening to 512-bit `vpshufb` in validation.
- Arms: `just bench-compare 6 utf8-to-utf16le utf16le-to-utf8` on commit `convert: add SIMD transcoders and length functions` (before the generic path's adaptive steps, which the native build does not run); simdutf 9.2.1 with its `icelake` kernel. Raw output in [convert-20261030/](convert-20261030/): `harness-benchstat.txt`, `harness-summary.txt`, `raw-*.txt`.
- Driver: 11 operations × 5 corpora (ascii = Latin-Lipsum, latin = French Wikipedia, cyrillic = Russian, cjk = Chinese-Lipsum, emoji = Emoji-Lipsum; simdutf's benchmark files, the latin1 corpus is French without characters above U+00FF) × 7 sizes from 16 bytes to 1 MiB, each cut to a character boundary; 7 interleaved samples per arm, pinned to CPU 2; medians in GB/s of input. `utf` is the checked form, `utf-valid` the valid form, `validate+utf-valid` simdutf's validator followed by our valid form (decision 4). simdutf is pinned to `haswell` against the AVX2 build and to `westmere` against the generic build, so each row compares like instruction sets. Tables: [avx2-vs-haswell.md](convert-20261030/avx2-vs-haswell.md), [generic-vs-westmere.md](convert-20261030/generic-vs-westmere.md).
- A/Bs: [ab-driver.zig](convert-20261030/ab-driver.zig) links two trees as `utf` and `old` into one binary, so arms interleave sample by sample.

## Takeaways

- **Against simdutf's AVX2 kernel** (`haswell`, 4 KiB–64 KiB, checked forms): UTF-8 to UTF-16 is ahead on ASCII (30.0 vs 18.9 at 4 KiB), Latin (3.9 vs 2.5), and emoji (1.48 vs 1.33), and at 82–85% on Cyrillic (1.90 vs 2.23) and CJK (2.54 vs 3.10). UTF-16 to UTF-8 is ahead on ASCII (39.7 vs 32.6), at 75–91% on Latin and Cyrillic, and at 51–55% on CJK (2.56 vs 4.65–5.01). UTF-32 to UTF-8 is 1.8× on emoji (3.57 vs 1.95) and 68% on CJK. UTF-32 to UTF-16 is 2× on emoji (7.46 vs 3.70), UTF-16 to UTF-32 1.45× (3.56 vs 2.45). Latin-1 to UTF-8 1.9× on ASCII (34.5 vs 18.3); UTF-8 to Latin-1 3.3× on Latin (10.8 vs 3.3). The length functions and `countUtf8` are 1.0–2.0× simdutf from 4 KiB, and 1.1–4.6× at 16 and 64 bytes.
- **Against simdutf's best kernel here** (`icelake`, AVX-512), harness run: geomean 5.91 vs 8.65 GB/s. Ahead on long ASCII UTF-8 to UTF-16 (+43% at 4 KiB, +62% at 64 KiB, +16% at 1 MiB) and on the Latin corpus (+11%); level on long ASCII and Latin UTF-16 to UTF-8; behind on every non-Latin script, 41–64% for UTF-8 to UTF-16 and 38–65% for UTF-16 to UTF-8, which is the AVX-512 kernel wave 2 owes. On short ASCII UTF-8 to UTF-16, simdutf leads at 64 bytes (6.55 vs ours 5.05) and std at 256 (13.9 vs 11.5); on UTF-16 to UTF-8 at 16 bytes, Rust (3.85 vs 2.78).
- **Short inputs**, AVX2 build: at 64 bytes we lead simdutf on every operation and corpus except the Latin rows of UTF-16 to UTF-8 (4.95 vs 5.68), UTF-32 to UTF-8 (9.7 vs 11.0), and UTF-32 to UTF-16 (7.7 vs 11.0); at 16 bytes on every row. std leads the 16-byte ASCII and Latin rows of UTF-16 to UTF-8 (8.65 vs 6.42) and UTF-8 to UTF-16 (2.86 vs 2.46).
- **Decision 4, checked vs validate-then-convert** (AVX2, `validate+utf-valid` arm): from 4 KiB, UTF-8 to UTF-16 checked is level with or ahead of simdutf's validator followed by our valid form on every corpus (ASCII 30.0 vs 19.7, Latin 3.89 vs 3.80, CJK 2.54 vs 2.51, emoji 1.48 vs 1.48), and UTF-16 to UTF-8 checked is ahead on all of them (Latin 16.2 vs 8.8, CJK 2.56 vs 2.18). Below that it holds except non-ASCII UTF-8 at 64 bytes, 10–12% behind (CJK 1.35 vs 1.53). The checked form costs 10–25% over the valid form on non-ASCII UTF-8, and about 2× on UTF-32 to UTF-16, whose valid loop is only a narrowing store (generic A/B tables).
- **ISAs**: SSE (`x86_64_v2`, [sse-vs-westmere.md](convert-20261030/sse-vs-westmere.md), measured before the switch to `simd.zig`'s helpers, which an A/B showed neutral on AVX2 and native) stands to simdutf's `westmere` as AVX2 does to `haswell`: ahead on ASCII, Latin-1, and UTF-32 sources, UTF-8 to UTF-16 CJK at 83%, UTF-16 to UTF-8 CJK at 58%, and UTF-16 to UTF-8 emoji at 70% (1.0 vs 1.43 in its own run, under scalar's 1.07). The generic path (`baseline`, SSE2 has no byte shuffle) vectorizes only ASCII and the shuffle-free kernels (UTF-16 to UTF-32 on BMP text, narrowing to Latin-1, Latin-1 to UTF-16/32, byte swap) and hands other blocks to the scalar code; see below. Against scalar in its table (a slow run of the orb; ratios only): ASCII and Latin are 2–30× scalar in every operation, UTF-16 to UTF-32 on BMP text 5–8×, the valid forms beat scalar on every corpus, and the checked forms are level to 25% behind scalar on text with no ASCII (UTF-8 to UTF-16 emoji 0.34 vs 0.46), the cost of the failed ASCII checks and the calls. Checked UTF-32 to UTF-16 is 12.7 GB/s to `westmere`'s 28.6 on BMP text.
- **Memory**: no allocation. Peak RSS in the harness equals std's (5.6–5.7 MiB, the inputs and output buffers); simdutf 8.3–8.4, Rust 7.9–8.9, Go 10.9–11.9. Stack use is the 64-byte blocks and 16-byte windows in registers. Comptime tables: 209×16 + 4096×2 bytes for UTF-8 windows, 625 + 256 + 256 entries of 17 bytes for packing to UTF-8, 256 + 16 + 16 entries of 16 bytes for compressing and pairing, about 35 KiB of rodata in all.
- **Correctness**: `just oracle 1000000` 0 mismatches for `convert.utf8ToUtf16le` (1,638,976 inputs) and `convert.utf16leToUtf8` (250,000), under simdutf's `icelake` and `fallback` kernels. A scratch fuzzer against libsimdutf (not committed; the oracle is the kept tool) compared every checked conversion and length function, error kind and position, on 200,000 random and adversarial inputs, with the library built for native, `x86_64_v2`, and `baseline`: 0 failures each.

## What moved the numbers

Interleaved A/Bs, kept only if they helped.

- Volatile loads of shuffle-table entries and of the 16-byte windows a shuffle uses in part: LLVM otherwise built them from narrower loads and inserts. Same finding as base64's.
- An all-lanes-below test (`v & ~(limit - 1)` reduced with OR) instead of `@reduce(.Max, v) < limit`, which compiled to a tree of shuffles.
- Keiser–Lemire validation shifts the lookup results by a lane (`simd.prev` on the first byte's lookups, with the previous block taken as ASCII, which holds because blocks start at character boundaries), not the input bytes: shifting the input made LLVM rebuild the block from overlapping loads.
- UTF-16 to UTF-8: test 32 units for ASCII before 16, then the one- and two-byte path, then the three-byte path without surrogates, and only then decode pairs. A 32-unit surrogate-free probe in UTF-16 to UTF-32 slowed emoji and was dropped.
- Short inputs: the UTF-8 block loop needs 64 bytes for an ASCII block and only falls to 16-byte windows under 80; the UTF-16 kernels need 16 units, 17 only to decode a pair.
- Length functions under 64 bytes, before and after ([short-lengths-before.md](convert-20261030/short-lengths-before.md), [short-lengths-after.md](convert-20261030/short-lengths-after.md)): 16-byte vectors with an overlapping masked last one, instead of the scalar loop. `utf16FromUtf8` at 64 bytes of Cyrillic 1.77 to 9.51 GB/s, `countUtf8` 2.46 to 12.1, `utf8FromUtf16le` on emoji 1.81 to 7.41, ASCII at 16 bytes 1.58 to 3.51; no row slower. Copying into a zeroed 64-byte stack block instead doubled the 64-byte rows but halved the 16-byte ones: the store-to-load forwarding stall costs about 17 ns.
- Generic path: emulating `pshufb` lane by lane lost to the scalar code on every non-ASCII corpus (UTF-8 to UTF-16 Cyrillic checked 0.30 vs scalar 0.45). Handing non-ASCII blocks to the scalar code ([generic-steps-vs-emulated.md](convert-20261030/generic-steps-vs-emulated.md), columns new, new-valid, old, old-valid): UTF-8 to UTF-16 checked Latin 0.40 to 1.12, emoji 0.13 to 0.26; UTF-16 to UTF-32 emoji 0.29 to 0.77; UTF-32 to UTF-16 emoji 1.17 to 1.78. Two details mattered. The scalar step runs between SIMD loops, not inside one: a call in the loop made LLVM spill its constants (ASCII rows −10–50%). And it is never inlined: inlined, LLVM merged its first byte store into the ASCII block store and rebuilt the block from bytes and shifts (Latin-1 to UTF-8 ASCII at half speed). Steps are 16 units after SIMD progress and double to 64 while blocks keep failing ([generic-adaptive-steps.md](convert-20261030/generic-adaptive-steps.md), against fixed 16): other scripts +10–45% (UTF-8 to UTF-16 valid emoji 1.26 to 1.61), Latin at 4 KiB −10–20%. The emulated kernel stays faster on valid CJK UTF-8 to UTF-16 (1.14 vs 0.43 GB/s) and UTF-32 (1.93 vs 0.90), and on UTF-16 to UTF-8 Cyrillic (0.65 vs 0.43).

## Wave 2

- AVX-512 kernels (VBMI2 compress, 64-byte windows): simdutf's `icelake` is 1.6–2.8× ours on non-Latin text.
- Against `haswell`: UTF-16 to UTF-8 on CJK (55%), UTF-32 to UTF-8 on CJK (68%), UTF-8 to UTF-16 on Cyrillic and CJK (82–85%); SSE UTF-16 to UTF-8 on emoji.
- Generic: the checked forms on text with no ASCII (up to 25% under scalar), checked UTF-32 to UTF-16 (44% of `westmere`), and keeping the emulated kernel where it wins (valid three-byte UTF-8, two-byte UTF-16), or decide runtime dispatch (decision 1), which makes most of this moot for x86-64.
- Short ASCII UTF-8 to UTF-16 against std (64–256 bytes) and UTF-16 to UTF-8 against std and Rust (16 bytes).

## ISA test matrix

`zig build test` (35 tests: every conversion's SIMD path against the scalar reference on valid text of six mixes at many lengths, exact-size and short output buffers, every error pattern at every position of inputs spanning several blocks for each source format, surrogate pairs split across blocks, and the length functions on arbitrary input), all passing:

| build                                                       | `simd.isa` | transcoding path                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `-Doptimize=Debug` (x86_64 self-hosted backend)             | avx512     | ASCII SIMD + scalar steps (no intrinsics) |
| `-Doptimize=ReleaseSafe` (native)                           | avx512     | AVX2 kernels, 512-bit `vpshufb` lookups   |
| `-Dcpu=x86_64_v3`                                           | avx2       | AVX2                                      |
| `-Dcpu=x86_64_v2`                                           | sse        | SSSE3                                     |
| `-Dcpu=baseline`                                            | generic    | ASCII SIMD + scalar steps                 |
| `-Dtarget=aarch64-linux-musl -fqemu`, Debug and ReleaseSafe | neon       | NEON `tbl`                                |

qemu-user is from nixpkgs (`qemu-user-11.1.2`). Mutants that broke the generic path's chunk boundaries (`utf8Boundary` not backing off continuation bytes, `utf16Boundary` splitting a pair) each failed two tests.
