# Base64 wave 2: AVX-512 VBMI, big-endian, dispatch, and tails, 2026-10-31

What wave 1 left in `src/base64.zig`: an AVX-512 VBMI kernel (Muła and Lemire, "Base64 encoding and decoding at almost the speed of a memory copy", 2020), the AVX2 decode gap, short inputs, whitespace (MIME lines), and five tests failing on aarch64_be. Also base64 behind runtime dispatch (plan decision 1), and two AVX-512 tail hazards the merged harness exposed.

- Machine: Amp orb, KVM guest, Intel Xeon @ 2.60GHz (Ice Lake server, family 6 model 106, AVX-512 with VBMI and VBMI2), 4 vCPUs, no PMU counters in the guest. Zig 0.16.0 ReleaseFast; simdutf 9.2.1 (nixpkgs); Rust 1.99.0 `base64-simd` 0.8.0 with `-C target-cpu=native`; Go 1.26.10.
- Two tools. The harness, `just bench-compare 6 <the seven base64 ops>` three times: native (our `avx512vbmi` kernel against simdutf's `icelake`), `cpu=x86_64_v3 dispatch=false kernel=haswell` (AVX2 against `haswell`), and `cpu=x86_64_v2 dispatch=false kernel=westmere` (SSSE3 against `westmere`); Rust, Go, and std are built for the host in all three. And the driver in [base64-20261031/driver.zig](base64-20261031/driver.zig): kernel against kernel by name (simdutf's through [shim.cpp](base64-20261031/shim.cpp)), the wave-1 tree (`old`, 7d7cb56) as before, std, 10 interleaved samples pinned to CPU 2, four operations: encode, decode, `lines` (76 columns, LF) and `crlf` (76 columns, CR LF). Its build and run commands are at its top.
- Throughput is input bytes: binary for encode, base64 characters for decode. Ratios below are medians of per-sample ratios from the same interleaved sample (`paired`), which are much tighter than benchstat's intervals on this orb, where one arm moves 10–40% between samples.
- The final tables are of `2e34c04` (the CR LF commit); the last commit, which only touches the short encode tail of the AVX2 and SSSE3 paths, has its own A/B below.

## Takeaways

- **Against simdutf at each ISA level, harness geomeans (utf ÷ simdutf):** native 0.98 overall, from 0.67 at the wave-2 baseline (encode 1.06, url 1.10; decode 1.00, url 0.93, strict 0.89, partial 1.00; MIME 0.92, from 0.50). AVX2 1.13 overall, from 0.99 (encode 1.29, 1.22; decode 1.13, 1.09, 1.12, 1.20; MIME 0.93, from 0.77). SSSE3 1.04 (MIME 0.92). The harness's base64 rows move ±15–40% between rounds; the driver's paired ratios below are the sharper measure.
- **Native, driver, utf ÷ `icelake`:** encode 0.80 at 16–32 B, 0.84 at 64, 0.91 at 256, then 1.09–1.19 from 1 KiB (46.2 against 40.3 GB/s at 4 KiB); decode 1.29–1.56 to 256 B, 1.17 at 1 KiB, 1.05 at 4 KiB, 0.99 at 64 KiB, 0.97 at 1 MiB; LF lines 1.04–1.42 except 0.94 at 1 MiB; CR LF lines 1.07–1.42 except 0.94 at 256 B and 1 MiB. Against wave 1: encode 1.01–2.39× (4 KiB 2.39×), decode 1.48–1.93× (64 KiB 1.65×), LF lines 1.46–1.96×, CR LF 1.51–2.04×.
- **AVX2, driver, utf ÷ `haswell`:** encode 1.01–1.85; decode 1.12–2.36 to 1 KiB and level above (0.98–1.00); LF lines 1.53–2.37 to 64 B, then 0.95, 0.87, 0.73, 0.70, 0.57 from 256 B to 1 MiB (8.2–8.4 against 11.4–12.1 GB/s at 4–64 KiB); CR LF 1.07–2.37 to 1 KiB, then 0.89, 0.87, 0.74. Against wave 1: encode 1.1–1.35× from 256 B, decode 1.17–1.63×, CR LF 1.16–1.64×; but encode at 16 B 0.84 and 64 B 0.92, and LF lines at 1 MiB 0.90.
- **SSSE3, driver, utf ÷ `westmere`:** encode 1.03–1.33 to 1 KiB, 0.94–0.99 above; decode 1.03–1.89 to 4 KiB, 0.99 at 64 KiB, 0.91 at 1 MiB; LF lines 1.39–1.90 to 64 B, 0.67–0.84 from 256 B; CR LF 0.81–1.0 from 256 B.
- **Rust and std, harness.** From 256 B up every row leads Rust's `base64-simd` (1.2–12× native, 1.1–6.4× in the AVX2 run, where Rust still targets the host) and std (3.0–34× native). The short rows are where they lead: decode at 16 B is 0.44–0.47 of Rust native (1.23 against 2.81 GB/s) and 0.54 in the AVX2 run, 0.63–0.80 of std; decode at 64 B 0.67–0.79 of Rust; encode at 16 B 0.83–0.96 of Rust and 0.86–0.91 of simdutf, and at 64 B 0.81–0.88 of simdutf native. The driver, which calls each arm through a function pointer, puts our 16 B decode at 1.9 GB/s (12.6 ns), 1.29× `icelake` and 1.18× std; in the harness, which inlines the call into its loop, it takes about 20 ns, while std takes 13–15 ns in both. Not found; this guest has no PMU, and the harness's 16-byte rows move ±30% between invocations.
- **Memory.** No allocation. Decode's whitespace stage is 648 bytes of stack (a 512-value ring), up from 336. Peak RSS in the harness is 3.4–3.7 MiB, 0.1–0.4 above std's (the AVX-512 kernel's code and tables) and 3.2–3.4 below simdutf's.

## What moved the numbers

Each was an interleaved driver A/B against the tree before it; raw output in [base64-20261031/](base64-20261031/).

- **Big-endian fix** (`ab-big-endian.txt`): geomean 0.99 over every kernel, op, and size; encode at 16–32 B reads 0.89–0.95 while the encode loop's machine code is unchanged, so layout. The failures on aarch64_be were byte-to-word lane bitcasts in encode's `translate` and decode's `pack`, not only bool-vector masks: both now use endian-aware shuffle masks and `simd.bitmask`, `simd.lookup16`, and `simd.load`, and NEON runs on aarch64_be.
- **AVX-512 VBMI kernel.** Encode is `vpermb`, `vpmultishiftqb`, `vpermb` per 48 bytes; decode is `vpermi2b` over a 128-entry table, then `vpmaddubsw`, `vpmaddwd`, `vpermb`; whitespace is removed with `vpcompressb` (VBMI2). Masked 48-byte stores in the loops were 9% slower than overlapping 64-byte ones.
- **Decode loop, two blocks per iteration with one bad-character test** (`ab-unroll.txt`): AVX-512 +10–11% from 1 KiB, AVX2 +4–10%.
- **AVX2 and SSSE3 encode** (`ab-encode-v3.txt`, `ab-encode-v2.txt`): the `pmullw` constant behind an empty asm, so LLVM does not turn the multiply into shifts and a blend, and the offset reduction `(idx -| 51) - (idx > 25)`: AVX2 +12–35% from 256 B, SSSE3 +11–19%; 16–64 B 0.80–0.97, from finishing short inputs in vectors.
- **Decode with fewer shuffles** (`ab-decode-v3.txt`, `ab-decode-v2.txt`): AVX2 stores each 16-byte half on its own instead of `vpermd`, and the special character's roll slot is `hi + eqmask`: AVX2 +8–19% from 256 B, SSSE3 +6–14%.
- **Whitespace ring** (`ab-ring-native.txt`): a staged block is decoded 256 values after it is written, so its load does not wait for the stores: native lines +11–13% from 4 KiB. **Shift masks from a table load** (`ab-shift-masks-v3.txt`): AVX2 lines +3–11% from 256 B.
- **AVX-512 tails without reading past the input** (`ab-tail-adjacent.txt`, `ab-tail-apart.txt`): with the output placed right after the input, as an arena or malloc does, the masked tail load waits for the last call's stores to the bytes it masks off: 16 B encode ran at 1.67 GB/s, wave 1's scalar tail at 2.90. Loading the 64 bytes that end at the input's end and moving them down with `vpermb`, or 16-byte and integer loads under 64 bytes: adjacent 1.3–2.0× (16 B encode 1.84×), apart 0.78–0.97 below 256 B. simdutf's `icelake` has the same stall (1.69 GB/s at 16 B adjacent).
- **AVX-512 tail stores masked only within a page** (`ab-store-fresh.txt`, `-adjacent`, `-apart`): a masked store whose masked-off bytes reach a page not yet written takes an assist on every call. In the harness, which maps each row's output fresh, 4 KiB decode ran at 25 GB/s against simdutf's 44 (and simdutf's own kernel does the same in the driver with a fresh buffer: both 21 GB/s, 40 once the buffer is touched). The tail store is now masked only when its 64 bytes stay on the page of its last byte, else 16-byte and integer stores: 4 KiB decode into a fresh buffer 1.71×, lines 1.33×, short rows level. The same commit computes the tail's encoded length without `encodedLen`'s options struct, which LLVM left in memory on the critical path.
- **CR LF in one shift** (`ab-crlf-v3.txt`, `ab-crlf-v2.txt`): a block whose two whitespace characters are adjacent shifts by two lanes once: AVX2 CR LF lines 1.30–1.35×, SSSE3 1.05–1.23×, LF level.
- **Short encode tail's options out of memory** (`ab-short-encode-v3.txt`, `-v2`, measured after the final tables): the scalar finish takes the alphabet's characters and the padding flag apart (`scalar.encodeChars`), since LLVM kept the inlined options struct in memory: AVX2 4–256 B 1.04× (16 B 1.05×), SSSE3 1.01×.
- **Dispatch**: `encode` and `decode` call `dispatch.call` with private `encodeKernel` and `decodeKernel`; `native` follows each tier's CPU. Not separately timed: native builds and `dispatch=false` call the kernel directly, as above.

## Correctness

- The tests run every kernel the build can run against the scalar reference, as wave 1 described, plus "encode and decode match the scalar reference on every tier", which runs the public functions through `dispatch.forEachLevel`; a bug planted in the AVX-512 kernel failed it in a baseline build.
- Gates on the final commit: `zig build test -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe` at `-Dcpu=native`, `x86_64_v3`, `x86_64_v2`, and `baseline`, 42 of 42 each; baseline Debug 42 of 42; `-Dtarget=aarch64-linux -fqemu` Debug and ReleaseSafe 41 passed and 1 skipped (the x86 tier test); `aarch64_be-linux` Debug and ReleaseSafe 39 passed, 1 skipped, and 2 failed: every base64 test passes, and transcoding's "checked conversions name the first error…" and "length functions count past their lane accumulators' range" failing (theirs, failing on main too); `just ci` exit 0.
- `just oracle` (1,000,000, seed 1): `base64.decode` 250,000 compared, 0 mismatches, under simdutf's `icelake` and its `fallback`; 0 mismatches in all.

## Gaps left

- AVX2 and SSSE3 whitespace from 4 KiB: LF lines 0.57–0.73 of `haswell`, 0.67–0.72 of `westmere`; CR LF 0.74–0.89. Each removal shifts the block with four shuffle-port instructions (`vperm2i128`, `vpermq`, two `vpalignr`) and two blends; simdutf compresses to a buffer with `pshufb` tables. The table compress was 35% slower than shifting here for one character.
- Short decode against Rust in the harness (16 B 0.44–0.54, 64 B 0.67–0.79), which the driver does not reproduce; short encode against simdutf `icelake` at 16–256 B (0.80–0.91 in the driver, 0.81–0.98 in the harness) and Rust at 16 B (0.83–0.86); AVX2 and SSSE3 encode at 16 B 0.84–0.88 of wave 1.
- 1 MiB, where memory bandwidth decides: native decode and lines 0.94–0.97 of `icelake`, SSSE3 encode and decode 0.91–0.94 of `westmere`.
